WR-Games is built on showing up — and your profile reflects that. Two systems track how you engage with the community: your **Trust Score**, which measures attendance reliability, and your **Reputation**, which captures the breadth of your contributions over time. Together they give other members and organizers a quick, honest read of who you are on the platform.

## Trust Score

Your Trust Score is a reliability rating on a **1.0–5.0 scale**, calculated from your attendance history. RSVP as Going and show up, and your score improves. Commit to an event and then don't appear, and it takes a hit.

Not every absence is treated the same. WR-Games weighs an absence by the strength of the commitment behind it:

- **You confirmed, then didn't come.** You answered Roll Call saying you'd be there and then didn't show. This carries the heaviest impact.
- **You cancelled after the event started.** Pulling your RSVP once an event is underway is recorded as an absence.
- **You never answered Roll Call.** An unconfirmed absence still counts, but it weighs less than a confirmed no-show.
- **A seat opened late and you couldn't take it.** If you were promoted from the waitlist, your score is protected.

We don't go into the details of how the score is calculated. It's built from real attendance patterns, and the principle is what matters: the stronger the commitment you made, the more an absence costs you.

<Note>
  Cancelling your RSVP **before an event starts** does not affect your Trust Score. Once the event has started, cancelling is recorded as an absence.
</Note>

### Why Trust Score Matters

Trust Score exists for the moment an organizer is looking at a list of names they don't recognize. When your event is open to the public, most of the people signing up aren't people you've gamed with before. Trust Score tells you which of them have a history of showing up for what they committed to.

That works both directions. When you ask to join a group, the organizer can look at your Trust Score and weigh it alongside everything else on your profile. A strong Trust Score signals that you're dependable, the kind of player people want to hold a seat for.

<Tip>
  The easiest way to protect your Trust Score is to respond to **Roll Call** four hours before an event. If something comes up and you can't make it, cancel your RSVP as early as possible so your spot can go to someone on the waitlist.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  Repeated no-shows can significantly lower your Trust Score. Organizers can see it when you ask to join their group or sign up for their events, so a low score can cost you a seat.
</Warning>

## Reputation (RP)

Reputation points (RP) reflect your overall engagement with the WR-Games community. Unlike Trust Score — which is purely about showing up — Reputation captures the many ways you contribute.

You earn RP from:

- **Attending events** — every event you attend adds to your RP total.
- **Hosting events** — running a session for others earns a bonus on top of attendance.
- **Receiving kudos** — when fellow players recognize your contribution, you earn RP.

### Reputation Levels and Dice Badges

As your RP grows, you advance through **50 numbered levels**. Each level carries a **dice badge** on your public profile, and the die changes shape as you climb — a d4 at the start, a d20 at the top.

| Your level | Badge die |
| --- | --- |
| 1–5 | d4 |
| 6–10 | d6 |
| 11–20 | d8 |
| 21–30 | d10 |
| 31–40 | d12 |
| 41–50 | d20 |

Your badge is a snapshot of how much you've engaged. A new member is still building a history. Someone who attends and hosts regularly becomes a recognized face in their groups. Further up, the badge reflects a long record of showing up, hosting, and earning kudos from other players.

## Kudos

After an event ends, attendees can give each other **kudos** — small recognition badges that call out things like great hosting, teaching a game patiently, or being a fantastic opponent. Kudos are a lightweight, friendly way to say "you made this session great."

<Accordion title="How do I give kudos?">
  After an event moves to **Past** status, open the event page and find the attendee list. Select any attendee and choose from the available kudos badges. You can give kudos to multiple people from the same event, but only once per person per event.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do kudos affect Trust Score?">
  No. Kudos contribute to your **Reputation (RP)** but have no effect on your Trust Score. Trust Score is based solely on attendance behavior.
</Accordion>

## Privacy Settings

You control whether your Trust Score and Reputation are visible on your public profile.

To hide either metric, go to **Settings → Privacy** and toggle off the relevant option. Other members — including organizers — will no longer see the values on your public profile.

<Info>
  Even if you hide your Trust Score publicly, organizers of groups you apply to or belong to may still be able to view it depending on the group's settings. Hiding your score does not reset or change the underlying value.
</Info>
